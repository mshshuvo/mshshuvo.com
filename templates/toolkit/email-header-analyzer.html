Paste the email's headers

The full header block, not just the From line. Nothing you paste is sent anywhere or saved.

Lets the tool spot look-alikes of your own domain, such as yourc0mpany.com. Only this is remembered on this device.

How to copy the full headers
Outlook (new and web)
Open the email, select the three dots (…), then View, then View message source. Copy everything above the first blank line.
Outlook (classic desktop)
Open the email, select File, then Properties. Copy the Internet headers box.
Gmail
Open the email, select the three dots next to Reply, then Show original. Copy the text from the top down to the first blank line.
Apple Mail
Open the email, then View, then Message, then All Headers (or Raw Source).
Thunderbird
Open the email, then View, then Message Source (Ctrl+U).

If the email is suspicious, copy the headers without clicking anything inside it. Pasting the whole source is fine: the tool ignores the message body.

The route it took

    What to do now

    • Don't click links, open attachments or reply until you're sure who sent it.
    • If it claims to be from someone you know, contact them a different way: a phone number you already have, or a new message you start yourself.
    • Report it with your mail app's Report phishing button, or forward it to your IT or security team with this summary.
    • If you already clicked a link or entered a password, change that password now and tell your IT team straight away.
    • In Canada you can also report fraud to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca).
    All headers

    Headers show who sent an email and how it travelled. They can't show whether a link or attachment is dangerous, and a message can pass every check and still be a scam (for example from a real but hacked account). The tool reads only what the headers say: it makes no DNS or reputation lookups.
