Ten emails. Real or phish?

Each round shows 10 emails that land in the inbox of Sam Taylor at a fictional company, Northgate Logistics. Some are genuine and some are phishing. Decide which is which.

  • Select any link or QR code to see where it really goes. Nothing opens, and nothing is clicked for real.
  • After each answer you'll see the signs that give it away, or that make it safe.
  • Every company and address is made up. A new mix of emails is picked each round.

The lesson from each email.

    Six signs of a phish

    Check these before you click, reply or pay.

    1. The real domain. Read the address from the right: the name just before the last dot is the real owner. northgate.example is real; northgate-helpdesk.example and northgate.example.msgcenter.example are not.
    2. Where links really go. Hover over a link (or press and hold on a phone) before you tap it. The text can say anything.
    3. Pressure. Deadlines, locked accounts and "within 24 hours" are there to stop you checking.
    4. Money or passwords. Gift cards, changed bank details, refunds, fees and "confirm your password" deserve a phone call first.
    5. Secrecy and odd channels. "Keep this between us", a personal Gmail for company business, replies that go somewhere else.
    6. Unexpected attachments and QR codes. .html, .zip and .iso files, and QR codes that ask you to sign in.

    Not sure? Check with the sender on a phone number you already have, and report it with your mail app's Report phishing button. Got the full headers? Run them through the Phishing Email Header Analyzer.
